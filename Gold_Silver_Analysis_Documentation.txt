Gold and Silver Correlation Analysis - Feature Documentation

1. Overview
The Gold and Silver Correlation Analysis is a full-stack machine learning feature designed to predict the future prices of Gold (GLD) and Silver (SLV) over a 10-year horizon, analyze their historical correlation, and provide deep mathematical transparency into how the prediction model makes its decisions.

2. Data Sourcing and Preparation (Backend)
- Data Source: The backend (Django/Python) fetches historical price data from the Yahoo Finance API (yfinance). It specifically pulls data for the 'GLD' (SPDR Gold Shares) and 'SLV' (iShares Silver Trust) ETFs to represent real-world asset prices.
- Aggregation: The daily price data is resampled into strictly 'Yearly' data, taking the final closing price of each year to create a smooth, long-term macroeconomic trendline.

3. Machine Learning Model & Feature Engineering
Instead of just using the 'Year' to predict prices, the model uses a multivariate approach to understand market momentum. 
The system trains two independent Scikit-Learn `LinearRegression` models (one for Gold, one for Silver) using 6 specific features:
    1. Year: The chronological year.
    2. GLD_Lag_1: The price of Gold exactly 1 year prior.
    3. SLV_Lag_1: The price of Silver exactly 1 year prior.
    4. GLD_Lag_2: The price of Gold exactly 2 years prior.
    5. SLV_Lag_2: The price of Silver exactly 2 years prior.
    6. Gold_Silver_Ratio_Lag_1: The mathematical ratio between Gold and Silver prices from 1 year prior (a classic precious metals trading indicator).

4. Walk-Forward Prediction (10-Year Forecast)
Because 5 of the 6 features rely on knowing the "previous year's price", the model cannot predict the year 2035 immediately. 
Instead, it uses a "Walk-Forward" prediction loop. It predicts the price for next year, and then feeds that newly predicted price backward into the model as the 'Lag_1' feature to predict the year after that. It iterates this process 10 times to generate a realistic 10-year forecast curve.

5. AI Explainability (SHAP and LIME)
To ensure the prediction isn't a "black box", the backend implements two enterprise-grade explainability frameworks:
- SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations): This evaluates the model globally. It calculates the average absolute impact (in dollars) that each of the 6 features has on the model's predictions across all years. This tells the user which features are the most "important" overall.
- LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations): This evaluates the model locally. It zooms in on a single specific prediction (e.g., the target year 2026) and breaks down exactly how much money each individual feature mathematically added or subtracted to reach that final predicted price.

6. Frontend Visualization (React)
The backend bundles the historical data, the 10-year predictions, and the SHAP/LIME explanations into a unified JSON payload (`gold_silver_data.json`) and serves it via a Django REST API endpoint.
The React frontend (`GoldSilverAnalysis.jsx`) consumes this API and generates several interactive charts using Recharts:
- Line Charts: Displays historical vs predicted trends for Gold and Silver independently.
- Scatter Plot: Shows the historical correlation mapping Silver against Gold, alongside the projected 10-year trend trajectory.
- Horizontal Bar Charts: Renders the SHAP global feature importances and the LIME local decision boundaries, color-coding positive (green) and negative (red) momentum contributions.
